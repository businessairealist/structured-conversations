# Journey Friction Rubric

Use this rubric when interpreting a completed customer journey map.

## High Friction

Treat a stage as high friction when one or more of these appear repeatedly:

- clear delays, waiting, or blocked progress
- confusion, uncertainty, or missing guidance
- repeated errors or failed actions
- extra manual steps or handoffs
- notes or ideas that explicitly call for training, clarification, or redesign

## Moderate Friction

Treat a stage as moderate friction when:

- the customer still completes the step, but the experience is awkward or slow
- needs and actual experience do not line up cleanly
- the stage creates avoidable support or staff effort

## Lower Friction

Treat a stage as lower friction when:

- the customer activity and expected experience align
- notes are mostly optimization ideas rather than problem signals
- the stage does not introduce meaningful uncertainty or delay

## Confidence Guidance

When evidence is thin:

- preserve the observed signal
- mark the ranking as heuristic
- add an open question instead of overstating certainty
