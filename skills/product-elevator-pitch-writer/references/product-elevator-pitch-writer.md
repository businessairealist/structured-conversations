# Product Elevator Pitch Writer Reference

## Purpose

Use this reference when drafting or updating a product elevator pitch artifact.

The goal is to package product value into a short, repeatable syntax that teams
can compare across personas without losing the customer problem or the product
differentiation.

## Canonical Seven-Line Syntax

Each persona column should fill these rows in order:

1. `For`
2. `Who`
3. `The`
4. `Is/Are`
5. `That`
6. `Unlike`
7. `Our Product`

Recommended interpretation:

- `For`: who the product is for or who benefits from it
- `Who`: the problem, need, or situation that matters to that audience
- `The`: the product name or offer name
- `Is/Are`: what the product actually is
- `That`: the main value or benefit
- `Unlike`: the current alternative, workaround, or competitor
- `Our Product`: the differentiator that makes the offer worth choosing

## Full Pitch Composition

Turn the filled lines into a short narrative:

`For <for> who <who>, <the> is <is/are> that <that>. Unlike <unlike>, our product <our product>.`

Allow light grammar cleanup when composing the final sentence. Preserve the
meaning of the individual lines.

## Render Guidance

The rendered output should mirror the workshop canvas style:

- left instructional guide panel
- row-label column with the seven pitch rows
- one completed column per persona
- a reusable blank template area for future persona variants

The image example for this skill is best interpreted as a layout target, not as
content to copy.

## Quality Rules

- Keep each row short enough to scan quickly in a workshop view.
- Prefer customer language over internal jargon.
- Do not invent strong claims without evidence.
- If the category, alternative, or differentiator is uncertain, record that as
  an open question.
- Treat upstream artifacts as evidence inputs, not as prose to paste verbatim.

## Evidence And Handoff Notes

Useful upstream evidence often comes from:

- empathy maps for pains, gains, and language cues
- journey maps for stage-specific friction
- impact maps for desired behavior change
- story maps for task and user context
- notes in `inputs/notes/` for stakeholder phrasing

The resulting artifact is often handed off to epic or story framing work. Keep
the problem, audience, and promised value explicit so downstream skills can
reuse them without re-deriving intent.
